The flock

Meet our sheep.

Three breeds, all chosen on purpose. Here's a bit about each one and what you can expect if you'd like to bring some home.

Valais Blacknose

The famously cute Swiss mountain sheep. Striking black face markings, soft curly wool and a calm, friendly nature.

  • Originally from the Swiss Alps, only arrived in Australia recently
  • Known for being calm, friendly and great around people
  • Wool is coarse and curly, often used for felting and rugs
  • We're part of the Australian breed-up program (see below)
Enquire about Valais

Babydolls

Small, smiley and ridiculously friendly. If you want a sheep that follows you around the paddock like a dog, this is the one.

  • Also called Olde English Babydoll Southdowns
  • Small breed: about 60cm tall at the shoulder
  • Great for small properties and gentle around kids
  • Soft fleece that hand spinners love
Enquire about Babydolls

Elliottdale

A rare Australian breed most people haven't heard of, and that's exactly why they matter. Very few breeding flocks left in the country.

  • An Australian breed, developed in Tasmania in the 1960s
  • Listed as endangered by the Rare Breeds Trust of Australia
  • Long, lustrous fleece prized by hand spinners
  • Hardy, easy keepers and good mums

For Valais buyers

What "breed-up" actually means.

If you've been reading about Valais Blacknose in Australia, you've probably seen the words "breed-up" and "F1, F2, F3" thrown around. Here's the plain version.

True purebred Valais Blacknose semen is imported from Europe and used on similar-looking ewes here in Australia. Each generation of lambs gets closer to a "full Valais":

  • F1: Half Valais, half base ewe
  • F2: Three quarters Valais
  • F3: Around 87% Valais
  • F4 and up: Considered fullblood for Australian registration

We're working through this program ourselves. When we have lambs available, we'll always tell you what generation they are and what their parentage looks like. No surprises.

Visit and buy

Thinking of getting some sheep?

Here's how it usually goes when someone enquires.

  • 1. Get in touch. Tell us a bit about your setup (property size, fencing, existing animals) and which breed you're interested in.
  • 2. Have a chat. We'll often pick up the phone or trade a few emails. It's not a sales pitch, just a conversation to make sure it's a good fit both ways.
  • 3. Come and visit. Meeting the sheep in person matters. You'll get a feel for our setup and we'll get a feel for what you're after.
  • 4. Choose your sheep. If you'd like to go ahead, we'll sort the paperwork (NLIS tags, vendor declaration, health declaration) and arrange a time for pickup.

A few practical notes

  • Boutique breeds aren't supermarket prices. Expect anywhere from $1500 to $3500+ depending on breed, age and quality.
  • We won't sell a single sheep on its own. They need at least one companion, ideally another sheep. If you don't already have one, we sell in pairs.
  • Pickup is from the farm. We don't currently offer transport but can suggest local livestock carriers.
  • Every sheep leaves with proper paperwork: NLIS eID tag, National Vendor Declaration and a Sheep Health Declaration.

If you've read this far and you're still keen, we'd love to hear from you. Drop us a line.
